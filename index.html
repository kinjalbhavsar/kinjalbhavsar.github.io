file 01

We've probably already met

Check the drawer where old phones go to sleep. If there's a Galaxy S3 or an HTC Evo in there, we've met — I helped bring those devices to life at Qualcomm, deep in the Android display stack, right as the platform was taking over the world.

The trail since then runs through array cameras that could refocus a photo after you took it, a nine-person startup putting Android on a real-time kernel for professional cameras, and Samsung's AR/VR lab. In 2019 it reached Google, where I spent four years on ARCore — including the camera work behind Project Guideline, which helps people with low vision run independently, and electronic image stabilization for AR.

file 02

Unlikely turns

Every resume is a straight line drawn after the fact. The actual route had detours.

megawatts

I grew up in power-plant townships across small-town Gujarat, son of a state electricity-board engineer. I've been at ease around big humming machines ever since.

the dentist

I came within a hair of dental school. Somewhere out there is a timeline where I'm reminding you to floss.

backwards

My first internship was porting an audio decoder from C++ into MATLAB. Yes, that direction. It taught me more than any tutorial ever did.

forbidden boot

Taiwan, 2007: I stitched Android's earliest kernel fork onto Qualcomm silicon that officially ran nothing but Qualcomm's own OS. It booted anyway.

worst timing

I landed in the U.S. in August 2008 — straight into the crash, when nobody was hiring. At my first career fair, a Qualcomm engineer read my resume and offered me a job on the spot. I turned down the job, kept the internship, and finished the master's.

la hoya

I pronounced La Jolla exactly the way it's spelled until a patient San Diego taxi driver staged an intervention.
