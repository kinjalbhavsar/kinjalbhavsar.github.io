01

Where it began

I was born in Borsad, a small town in Gujarat, India, and grew up moving between power-plant towns — Ukai, then Panandhro out in the Kutch desert, and finally Wanakbori, at the time the largest power station in the state. My father was a mechanical engineer for the state electricity board, so home was always a company colony where you knew just about everyone.

That small-town warmth stuck with me — and so did a stubborn streak. My family hoped I'd become a doctor; I came within a hair of dental school. But a string of happy accidents pushed me toward computers instead. I started in Computer Engineering at Nirma, loved it, and then a late-admissions reshuffle and some Y2K-era nerves about job security nudged me into Electronics & Communication at DDIT in Nadiad.

DDIT had a reputation for being relentless — weekly exams, a brutal semester pace — and it made me gritty and resourceful. It's also where I fell for programming for good, and made the kind of friends you keep for life.

I grew up knowing pretty much the whole town. That warmth never really left me.

02

Learning the craft

My first real job was at eInfochips in Ahmedabad, writing embedded software. My internship there was gloriously backwards — porting an open-source MPEG-4 AAC decoder from C++ into MATLAB — and from there I cut my teeth for real: optimizing kernels for TI's VLIW DSPs and writing Linux audio drivers for DaVinci hardware.

In late 2007 I moved to Taiwan to bring Linux up on Qualcomm's then-brand-new MSM chips — back when Qualcomm only officially blessed its own OS. I stitched their bootloader together with Android's earliest kernel fork and got it booting for a Foxconn subsidiary. I learned an enormous amount — and I learned that I wanted to study more.

So I took the GRE, got into NC State, and landed in Raleigh in August 2008 — straight into the financial crisis. It was a frightening time to arrive: no one was hiring, and my dad had dipped into his retirement savings to send me. Then, at my very first career fair, a Qualcomm engineer read my resume and offered me a job on the spot. (That summer also took me to a San Diego neighborhood called La Jolla, which I confidently mispronounced until a patient taxi driver set me straight.)

I turned the job down to finish my master's — but took the internship. I think I was the only one in my class to land one that year, in the middle of the recession.
03

Building for billions

I joined Qualcomm in San Diego in 2010, working on the Android display stack just as the platform was exploding. I got to help bring up devices I'm still proud of — the Galaxy S3, the HTC Evo — and somewhere in there, code I'd touched started running on 2+ billion devices.

Then came the startup years: Pelican Imaging, chasing depth-sensing array cameras a little ahead of their market, and mPerpetuo, where I was employee number nine trying to run Android on a real-time kernel for professional cameras. After a stint in Samsung's AR/VR lab, I finally cleared the bar at the one company I'd been chasing for years.

Google was the one I kept missing. I didn't pass twice — so when I finally did, I took a downlevel just to get in the door.

I joined ARCore in 2019, and today I build Android XR — Jetpack XR SceneCore, the SDK developers use to make spatial, 3D apps. The work lives here, if you want the receipts:

04

Life beyond the editor

I met Hiral in 2011 — she was in the Bay Area at Cisco — and after a year of dating we married in early 2012 and I moved north. We're raising two kids now, a daughter and a son a couple of years apart, in a Fremont house we picked mostly for the schools.

I'm a textbook Gemini: I live for new places, new food, new people. My wife is my opposite — happiest at home with family — and our kids split the difference (my son's the adventurer, my daughter takes after her mom). Between us we've chased national-park stamps from Yosemite to Yellowstone to Zion, and dragged the kids everywhere from Hawaii to Japan to London.

On weekends you'll also find me coaching a FIRST Lego League team through the Submerged season — building and programming robots with the next generation of engineers. Mostly, though, I'm just trying to be a better dad than I was yesterday.
